MEASURE WITH CONTEXT

From pixels to motion.

A practical guide to the FaceScope analysis pipeline.

01

Choose a source

Start your webcam or open an MP4, WebM, or another video your browser supports. Keep one face visible, with even lighting. Videos remain in browser memory; frames are processed locally.

Live playback samples at the selected maximum rate. “Analyze full video” seeks through the entire file at that rate and creates a new dataset. This can detect short events more reliably than slow live inference, but very short blinks may still be missed.

02

Set a neutral reference

Face forward, keep your eyes open, and press Set neutral. Hold still for 30 tracked samples. Median eye opening sets personalized blink thresholds; pose and iris medians define zero offsets for the display.

Exports always retain absolute raw measurements. Display smoothing affects the live cards only. Calibration and settings are included in session JSON.

03

Read the measurements

Eye aspect ratio: two eyelid distances divided by twice the eye-corner distance. Pixel coordinates account for video aspect ratio. A blink requires both eyes to close, then reopen within 0.05–0.8 seconds.

Mouth opening: inner-lip height divided by mouth width. Head orientation: approximate Euler angles from the model transform. Iris position: position along the eye-corner axis and perpendicular offset, measured in eye-width units.

04

Save, export, reproduce

CSV includes timestamps, measurements, and all returned blendshape coefficients. JSON also includes events, settings, and optional landmark arrays. The HTML report summarizes the dataset and prints to PDF through your browser.

Saved sessions use a local SQLite database. Export or delete them from Saved sessions. Camera frames and source video files are never written by the session service.

What the model can and cannot tell you

The 478 landmarks and 52 blendshape coefficients describe estimated face geometry and visible movement. Blendshapes are animation coefficients, not probabilities of emotions. Iris position is not calibrated gaze, and the mesh is not a metric 3D scan. Glasses, reflections, occlusion, edits, embedded overlays, extreme pose, and poor lighting can reduce tracking quality.

FaceScope does not identify a person or infer their feelings, attention, honesty, health, or diagnosis. “Tracking coverage” is the percentage of sampled frames containing a detected face, not an accuracy score. For validation, use annotated footage and compare detected events against ground truth.
